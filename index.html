In Our Own Words

The world is changing, and it is changing fast. Many organisations are still relying on legacy methods to set objectives, measure performance, and define improvement pathways.

Definitions of value are shifting from purely financial outcomes toward sustainability and social acceptability. We understand short-term objectives, while recognising they are not always reconcilable with longer-term goals. Short-term EBITDA gains can erode company and process-specific knowledge built over years.

Most harmful in value creation is direct application of historical data to define future ambition. In today's dynamic environment, leaders must define where they need to be, then backcast milestones for performance setting. This immediately exposes the need to track value-creating actions separately from traditional EBITDA bridge reporting. Why? Standard bridges can hide leakage from inefficiency, mistakes, and projects that are no longer fit for purpose. AT-IPIC changes that performance landscape.

The 2 Business Focus Areas

Planning & Execution — in Harmony

"In a fast-moving world, a system fixed to a financial reporting scheme won't be flexible and dynamic. These two focus areas can run at different speeds as needed and empower good, timely decision making."

📐

Planning

The Planning focus area identifies opportunities and drives strategic direction. It captures ideas, innovation pathways, and potential actions — providing the forward-looking perspective that keeps your organisation ahead of change rather than reacting to it.

Transformation captures ideas, innovation and actions. Opportunity management provides direction and decision-making insight.

⚡

Execution

The Execution focus area translates strategic intent into operational reality — tracking actions, managing the project portfolio, and ensuring that value created in planning is actually realised on the ground.

We don't just plan — we track, measure, and ensure delivery, making certain that EBITDA gains are captured, not lost to leakage and operational instability.
