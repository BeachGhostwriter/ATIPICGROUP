Neptune

The AT-IPIC water intelligence platform — water balance, treatment selection, and cost-of-value analysis in one decision tool.

What It Does

Water Decisions, Grounded in Data

Neptune models water balance across a site, evaluates treatment options against cost and performance, and translates the result into a clear investment and sustainability case — replacing scattered engineering estimates with one consistent analytical tool.

Built for industrial water managers, Neptune connects operational reality — flows, quality, and treatment capacity — directly to the financial and value-creation metrics that decisions actually get made on.

Try It

Build a Treatment Train

Follow water through each stage of treatment, choose the technology for every stage and see how the choice changes the plant and its energy footprint.

1

Choose a technology per stage

Below the animation, pick one option for each of the six stages — pre-treatment, primary, secondary, tertiary, concentrate management and the zero-liquid-discharge (ZLD) end point. Options run from basic to advanced; hover an option for what it does.

2

Watch the plant rebuild

The scene rebuilds immediately for the combination you choose. Press play, or use the station dots, to follow the water from intake through treatment, polishing or recycling, to brine concentration and salt recovery.

3

Read the energy footprint

Each technology has a specific energy demand in kWh per m³. Neptune multiplies it by the flow that stage actually treats and by the operating hours. Concentrate stages only see the much smaller brine flow, and a crystallizer only the residue left after concentration. An anaerobic MBR returns energy as biogas.

4

Compare combinations

Swap one stage at a time to see where the energy goes — for example, replacing thermal evaporation (MEE) with BrineX membrane + thermal cuts the concentrate stage by about 40%. In the full platform the same choices drive CAPEX, OPEX, carbon, EBITDA and your site's own water balance.

Energy (kWh/yr) = specific energy (kWh/m³) × stage flow (m³/h) × operating hours (h/yr), summed over all six stages.
